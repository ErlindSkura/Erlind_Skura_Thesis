\chapter{CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Conclusion}

This thesis addressed the automated quantification of beads in SEM
micrographs of electrospun nanofibre mats, a measurement currently performed by
hand and therefore slow and difficult to reproduce.

The dataset is reported in Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}: eleven micrographs of
four specimens at three magnifications, carrying 606 manually delineated beads,
characterised and placed on a physical scale from each micrograph's burned-in
scale bar, with the calibration validated by the median equivalent bead diameter
agreeing to within 15\% across the three magnifications. Two properties determine
how the data have to be used. The 143 image--annotation pairs supplied are 11
originals and 132 transformed copies, so the effective sample size is 11 and the
copies cannot cross a partition boundary; and the eleven micrographs derive from
only four specimens, so the specimen rather than the image is the unit of
partitioning. Both are reflected in the leave-one-specimen-out protocol.

Under that protocol, Mask R-CNN outperforms the mask-predicting alternatives on
every metric, reaching $\mathrm{AP}_{50} = 0.298 \pm 0.059$, an Aggregated Jaccard
Index of $0.325 \pm 0.053$ and a mean absolute counting error of 41.2\%. It
reaches neither success criterion of Section~\ref{sec:objectives}, falling far
short of the $\mathrm{AP}_{50} \geq 0.70$ recorded at the proposal stage and two
thousandths short of the 0.30 a standard detector reaches on a benchmark matched
to this object size, the latter a figure measured on boxes, which the two
detection-only methods do clear at 0.305 and 0.315. Its decomposed Panoptic
Quality, 0.719 against 0.486, locates the deficit: the model delineates well the
beads it finds and finds fewer than half of them. Scored on the micrographs it
trained on it reaches 0.437, so the shortfall is one of capacity and training
budget rather than of generalisation.

The comparison against the five alternatives answers the question the thesis was
built around, and each fails in a way that isolates a different component. The
U-Net merges 48.7\% of beads and never splits one, so it under-counts by 44\% with
a bias that never changes sign, while its mask accuracy differs from Mask R-CNN's
by only 0.020 in AJI: what the instance formulation contributes is the separation
of objects that touch. Faster R-CNN, which is Mask R-CNN with the mask branch
removed, counts as well as the full model, 39.5\% against 41.2\%, for 15\% less
training time, so the mask branch is not what makes the count work but what makes
the size distribution measurable at all. YOLOv8 finds more of the real beads than
any other method, a recall of 0.696 against 0.447, while reporting 1223 objects
against 606 annotated for a counting error of 99.3\%. YOLOv5u reaches the highest
box $\mathrm{AP}_{50}$ in the study, $0.315 \pm 0.049$, and the highest
$\mathrm{AP}_S$, 0.132, counting three specimens to within 8.2\%, 15.0\% and
18.3\% while reporting 591 objects for the 238 beads of the fourth, an error of
212.5\% traceable to confidence scores that are not comparable across
magnifications within a specimen. The classical Otsu-plus-watershed pipeline fails
outright at $\mathrm{AP}_{50} = 0.001$, because beads are separated from the
surrounding mat by only about 20 grey levels.

Three findings were not anticipated. Detection improves monotonically with
magnification, from $\mathrm{AP}_{50} = 0.227$ at $500\times$ to 0.396 at
$3000\times$, even though that group holds only 52 of the 606 beads, so object
size in pixels rather than the number of training examples is the binding
constraint; magnifying the dataset threefold raises $\mathrm{AP}_{50}$ to 0.373
and recall to 0.655 without adding information, so the gain is itself the
evidence. And the naive partitioning protocol did not measurably overstate
performance, a random split giving 0.303 against 0.298 against a between-fold
standard deviation of 0.059, although it produced a fold-to-fold spread 1.7 times
as wide. A fourth result is negative in a narrower sense: none of the three
preprocessing transforms improved detection, Mask R-CNN scoring 0.298 on raw
micrographs against 0.289, 0.263 and 0.262, while background subtraction cut
counting error from 41.2\% to 31.5\%.

\section{Research Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item A characterised and physically calibrated dataset of 606 beads
    across 11 SEM micrographs of four electrospun specimens, with per-magnification
    pixel sizes recovered from the instrument's own scale bars and validated for
    internal consistency to within 15\%.

    \item An evaluation protocol matched to the hierarchical structure of small
    microscopy datasets, in which the specimen is the unit of partitioning,
    together with a measurement of what a naive split actually costs. That
    measurement returned no resolvable difference in the mean and a fold-to-fold
    spread 1.7 times as wide, which is a more useful result than the inflation
    that had been expected.

    \item A controlled comparison of six methods on identical micrographs under an
    identical protocol, isolating object separation rather than mask accuracy as
    what instance-level modelling contributes on this modality, quantified as a
    23.8 percentage point difference in merge rate, and isolating the mask branch
    as worth every mask-level measurement and nothing at all on the count.

    \item A measurement of preprocessing as an experimental factor rather than a
    default, on a material whose background carries structure at the same spatial
    scale as the objects, showing that the transform which most improves the raw
    bead-to-mat separation is the one that most harms detection.

    \item A demonstration that the binding constraint is pixels per object rather
    than the number of annotated objects, obtained by magnifying the dataset
    threefold and holding everything else fixed, and separately by scoring each
    fold's model on its own training partition and finding a model that under-fits.

    \item An open implementation covering annotation conversion, scale
    calibration, training, evaluation and the reporting of bead count, areal
    density and size distribution in physical units, in which every table in
    Chapter~\ref{ch:results} is generated from the stored metrics rather than
    transcribed, and whose complete set of results regenerates in 10.8 hours on a
    single freely available GPU.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}

The limitations are set out in full in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}: four specimens of a single material system, 52 annotated beads at the highest magnification, a single annotator, a single annotation class, no hyperparameter search, two one-stage methods without a matched input budget, and a single training run per configuration at a fixed seed.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:futurework}

\subsection{Short-term}

\textbf{Address the recognition deficit directly.} A Segmentation Quality of 0.719
against a Recognition Quality of 0.486 indicates that effort should go into
finding beads rather than refining masks. The first steps are a longer schedule
than the 1500 iterations the available GPU time allowed, a hyperparameter search
this work did not perform, and a loss that up-weights recall; the model scores
0.437 on data it has already seen, so this is the right place to spend. The recall
of 0.696 obtained by YOLOv8 gives the target.

\textbf{Calibrate the score threshold per magnification, not per fold.} This is
the cheapest experiment suggested by any result here and the one with the largest
measured headroom. The single catastrophic fold of YOLOv5u is mostly an operating
point, since one threshold was selected for a specimen whose three magnifications
need different ones, and that fold's error halves at the value the other folds
chose. Selecting per magnification group, still on training data only, is a change
of a few lines and costs no additional training.

\textbf{Suppress false positives rather than chase recall.} The recall advantage
of YOLOv8 is bought with 801 spurious detections. A post-hoc classifier over
detections would test whether the recall of one method and the precision of
another can be obtained together.

\textbf{Extend the specimen count.} The most valuable addition is more specimens
rather than more images of the same ones. Four support four folds; ten would
support a materially stronger claim and would give the protocol comparison of
Section~\ref{sec:leakage} the resolution these four folds lacked.

\textbf{Measure inter-annotator agreement.} A second annotator re-delineating a
subset would convert the largest stated limitation into a measured quantity and
establish the ceiling against which any model should be judged, since at
$\mathrm{AP}_{50} = 0.298$ it is not known how much of the shortfall is model
error and how much is disagreement a second human would also show.

\textbf{Use the unannotated micrographs.} If further micrographs exist without
annotations, they can be used for self-training or consistency-based
semi-supervised learning, both well suited to a regime with abundant images and
scarce labels.

\textbf{Shape-prior architectures.} Beads are approximately ellipsoidal and
convex, which is exactly the assumption behind StarDist \cite{schmidt2018stardist}
and the flow-based representation of Cellpose \cite{stringer2021cellpose}. Both
are designed for the touching-object problem Chapter~\ref{ch:results} identifies
as the central difficulty.

\subsection{Long-term}

\textbf{Image at the magnification the measurement needs.} Higher magnification
gives better boundaries, while a count is only as good as the number of objects in
the field, so a mixed protocol, a wide field for density and several high-power
fields for size, may measure both better than any single setting. Testing it
requires micrographs acquired for the purpose.

\textbf{Joint fibre and bead analysis.} Bead quantification is one half of the
morphological characterisation of a nanofibre mat, the fibre diameter distribution
being the other, which tools such as DiameterJ \cite{hotaling2015diameterj}
address separately. A single model reporting both from one micrograph would cover
what a laboratory actually needs.

\textbf{From measurement to process feedback.} If bead statistics could be linked
to the spinning parameters that produced them, the pipeline would move from
characterisation towards prediction, estimating from a micrograph which parameter
is out of range. This requires micrographs labelled with their process conditions,
which the present dataset does not carry.

\textbf{Annotation assistance from foundation models.} The Segment Anything Model
\cite{kirillov2023segment} cannot recognise beads on its own, but prompted with a
detector's boxes it could refine mask boundaries, or accelerate the annotation of
new specimens by turning a click into a polygon. Given that annotation effort is
the binding constraint, this is the intervention with the highest leverage.

\section{Final Remarks}

The pipeline built here does not yet replace the operator with the mouse. At an
$\mathrm{AP}_{50}$ of 0.298 and a per-micrograph counting error ranging from
$+75\%$ to $-119\%$, no laboratory should report its output on a single field as a
measurement without checking it.

What the work does establish is where the difficulty lies, which is worth more at
this stage than a higher number would have been. It is not segmentation accuracy,
since the masks overlap their targets by 0.72 on average, and it is not the size
distribution, since the predicted median diameter agrees with the manual
measurement to 3.2\%. It is the detection of small, low-contrast objects that
touch one another, and the measurements that isolate it are the
recognition-versus-segmentation split of Panoptic Quality, the merge and split
rates, the magnification trend, and the threefold resampling that reproduces that
trend without adding a single new observation.

One methodological result deserves to outlive the specific numbers: the ranking of
the methods depends on which quantity is asked for. YOLOv8 beats the U-Net on
average precision and loses to it on counting. YOLOv5u posts the best detection
scores together with a counting error that is either the best measured or the
second worst, depending on which specimen is held out. The two methods agreeing
best with the manual size distribution are the two that over-count most severely.
A study reporting a single accuracy figure would have ranked the methods wrongly
for the laboratory that has to use them, and would not have been able to tell.

Three of the results are negative: the accuracy criterion was not met, the
data-leakage effect the protocol was designed to prevent did not appear, and no
preprocessing transform improved detection. All are reported as measured, and the
second is the more useful finding, since it tells the next person working on a
dataset of this shape that the expensive precaution matters for the variance of
their estimate rather than for its mean.
